# Insight — Plain-Language Guide

**What this is:** a simple guide to what Insight does and how to use it. It is written for people who are not technical — teammates, demo audiences, stakeholders, or anyone who wants to understand the product without reading code.

**The short version:** Insight is a workspace that helps a busy team find the one conversation that matters most, instead of scrolling through everything.

---

## 1. What Insight is

Insight is like a **daily briefing for a team’s conversations**.

Teams talk in a lot of places: chat channels, documents, project updates, code changes. Important decisions get buried under everyday noise. Insight collects those conversations in one place and points to the one thing that deserves attention right now.

That one thing is called the **signal**.

> **Every thread. One clear signal.**

Instead of asking “What did I miss?”, you open Insight and see:

- What changed
- What was decided
- What is blocked
- What needs attention next

---

## 2. The problem it solves

Imagine a team with hundreds of messages, several shared documents, and a pile of project updates.

The important question is usually simple:

> “What actually matters today?”

But finding the answer means reading a lot of context, checking several places, and asking colleagues what happened. That takes time and attention.

Insight does the reading for you and gives you a short, clear answer. It is not meant to replace conversation. It is meant to help you catch up quickly and get back to work.

---

## 3. What makes it different

Most tools give you **more** to read.

Insight tries to give you **less** — but better.

| Most tools | Insight |
| --- | --- |
| Show every message | Shows the one signal that matters |
| Ask you to search | Gives you a summary with sources |
| Add another feed | Protects your attention |
| Tell you what happened | Helps you decide what to do next |

The goal is not to answer more questions. The goal is to give you back your attention.

---

## 4. Who it is for

Insight is designed for teams that would rather build than scroll:

- Product and design teams
- Engineering teams
- Founders and small companies
- Remote or distributed teams
- Anyone who has to catch up after time away

It is especially useful when someone has been offline, joined a project late, or needs to understand a decision without reading the entire history.

## 7. A tour of the workspace

When you sign in, the screen has three main areas.

### 7.1 The left sidebar

This is your control panel. It contains:

- **Thread filters** — All threads, Chat, Doc, Commit, with a count next to each
- **+ New thread** — add a conversation or update
- **Run signal scan** — re-run the demo analysis
- **Export CSV** — intended to download a spreadsheet (see the note below)
- **Last scan** — a small summary of the most recent scan
- **Your account** — your name, email, and Sign out
- **Connection status** — a small dot showing whether the app is connected

### 7.2 The signal card

This is the centerpiece. It shows the single most important thread right now.

You will see:

- A short **headline** describing the signal
- A sentence explaining **why it matters**
- A **score** showing how strongly it ranked
- How many **threads were read** to produce it
- A button to **rescan**

Think of it as the answer to: “If I only read one thing today, what should it be?”

### 7.3 The thread list

Below the signal is a list of conversations. Each card shows:

- The **type**: Chat, Doc, or Commit
- The **title**
- The **source**, such as a channel or document name
- The **urgency**: Low, Medium, High, or Critical
- When it was last **updated**

Click a thread to open it. Use the filters to narrow the list.

## 8. Your first five minutes

If you want a quick, guided demo, follow this path.

### Step 1 — Sign in

Use the demo account shown on the sign-in screen.

### Step 2 — Read the signal

Look at the large card at the top. Read the headline and the explanation underneath.

Ask yourself: “Does this match what a busy team would need to know first?”

### Step 3 — Try a filter

In the sidebar, choose **Chat**, **Doc**, or **Commit**. Notice how the list changes and the count updates.

### Step 4 — Open a thread

Click any thread card. A panel slides in from the right.

You will see:

- A short summary
- Key facts such as source, urgency, score, and last update
- The **message trail** — the conversation that led to this point
- A box to add a reply

### Step 5 — Add a reply

Type a short message and send it. The trail updates with your message. This shows how Insight keeps the story behind a thread.

### Step 6 — Add a thread

Close the panel, then choose **+ New thread**.

Fill in:

- Type (Chat, Doc, or Commit)
- Title
- Source
- Summary
- Urgency

Depending on the type, you may also see a few simple number fields, such as message count, sections, files changed, or additions.

Save it, and the new thread appears in the list.

### Step 7 — Run a scan

Choose **Run signal scan** in the sidebar.

The app briefly shows that it is scanning, then updates the signal and the “Last scan” summary. This is a demonstration of how Insight refreshes its view of the workspace.

### Step 8 — Try a smaller screen

If you can, resize the browser window or open the app on a phone. The layout adapts: the sidebar moves above the content, and thread cards simplify.

## 9. The three thread types

Insight uses three simple categories.

### Chat

A conversation or channel discussion.

Examples:

- A product channel reacting to new pricing
- A support conversation about a customer issue
- A team discussing a launch

### Doc

A document, brief, plan, or written update.

Examples:

- A launch brief
- A research summary
- A decision record

### Commit

A change to a project, such as code or a shared file.

Examples:

- A feature landing
- A bug fix
- A configuration change

The type is shown as a small badge on each thread. The badge helps you understand the kind of work at a glance.

## 10. Urgency and score, in plain language

### Urgency

Urgency tells you how quickly something may need attention.

| Level | What it suggests |
| --- | --- |
| **Low** | Informational; no immediate action |
| **Medium** | Worth reviewing soon |
| **High** | Needs attention; could affect work |
| **Critical** | Needs attention now |

Urgency is a signal, not a command. Use your judgment.

### Signal score

The score is a ranking number. A higher score means the thread is more likely to deserve attention.

The score considers:

- How urgent the thread is
- How much activity it has, such as messages, sections, or changes

You do not need to calculate it. It is there to explain why one thread rose to the top.

## 13. What is still a demo

These points matter if you are showing Insight to someone or evaluating it.

### The workspace is shared

Everyone who signs in sees the same sample workspace. There are no separate private workspaces or team accounts yet.

### The scan is a demonstration

“Run signal scan” simulates analysis. It does not connect to real chat apps, documents, or project tools. It is showing what the experience would feel like.

### The early-access form is cosmetic

On the marketing website, the email form shows a success message, but it does not actually send or save your email address.

### Export CSV does not currently download

The button is visible, but the download does not work in the browser today. Avoid relying on it during a demo.

### Sign-ins are temporary

If the demo server restarts, everyone is signed out. There is no password reset or account recovery flow.

### There are no real notifications

Insight does not send emails, alerts, or push notifications. The workspace refreshes on its own about every 20 seconds.

### The marketing copy is aspirational

The website describes a fuller product than the demo currently provides, including real integrations and enterprise controls. Treat those as the product vision, not current features.

### Do not use sensitive information

This is a demonstration environment. Use sample content, not confidential team data.

## 14. Tips for showing Insight

If you are demonstrating the product to someone:

1. **Start with the problem.** “Teams have too much to read and not enough time.”
2. **Sign in with the demo account.** Let the sample workspace appear.
3. **Read the signal out loud.** Point out the headline and the reason underneath.
4. **Show the thread behind it.** Open the thread and scroll through the message trail.
5. **Add a reply.** This makes the product feel like a real workspace, not a static mockup.
6. **Run a scan.** Explain that this is how Insight refreshes its view.
7. **End on the promise.** “Every thread. One clear signal.”

Avoid spending time on the Export CSV button or the early-access form unless you are explicitly discussing what is still unfinished.

## 15. If something looks wrong

| What you see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| “API offline” or a red status dot | The demo is not connected to its data service. Ask whoever is hosting the demo to restart it. |
| “No signal yet” | The workspace has no threads. Add one, or use the demo account. |
| An error message when the workspace is empty | This can happen on a brand-new workspace. Adding a thread usually clears it. |
| The Export CSV button does nothing | This is a known limitation, not something you did wrong. |
| You were signed out unexpectedly | The demo service may have restarted. Sign in again with the demo account. |
| A thread’s urgency, time, or delete control is missing on a phone | The mobile layout hides those details to keep the card readable. |
